\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

We formalise one question. \emph{Do large software systems converge in their high-level
abstractions, structures and architectures, in the way that low-level ABIs, calling
conventions and stack frames converge?} The intuition is decomposed into three
propositions of differing strength, each given a decidable or falsifiable form.

Four theses organise the work. The \term{compression thesis}: a complex system is
comprehensible to a bounded intelligence exactly to the extent that it is compressible;
comprehension is task-constrained lossy compression whose optimal descent depth is the
stationarity condition of a rate--distortion Lagrangian. The \term{necessity thesis}: any
system with concurrently live entities and contended finite resources, whose operational
semantics is well defined, has already defined six functions---identity and lifecycle,
allocation and reclamation, scheduling, naming and addressing, communication and
synchronisation, and fault isolation and recovery; these may be built or adopted, but not
omitted---and the claim is a checklist obligation, not a claim that the six are minimal or
mutually independent. The \term{separation thesis}: generation may be free, probabilistic and
untrusted, while admission must be deterministic, mechanical and exhaustively inspectable;
formal-layer
guarantees come from a minimal trusted kernel, intent-layer guarantees from adversarial
consensus, and the two are stitched by artefact binding. The \term{design thesis}:
architecture is manufactured separation of scales---nature obtains levels free through
renormalisation, software must pay for them---and design is forward compression while
comprehension is inverse compression, two reading directions of one structure.

The formal results are of three different kinds, labelled as such throughout, and the
distinction is part of the contribution. As \emph{working definitions}: a level's
\emph{oblivious set} as the kernel of its abstraction map, which turns ``what this level may
ignore'' into an object that can be declared and then checked---declared rather than extracted,
because the definition confers no computability and the checks against it are partial; a
\emph{contract operad} whose side conditions locate exactly where composition fails, including
the case of resource-coupled substitution that neither colouring nor acyclicity detects; expected
read-set as a measurable architectural objective; and near-decomposability as interface
sufficiency, yielding a differential diagnostic for \emph{leak pairs}. As \emph{conditional
results}: composition of inter-level soundness under observation compatibility and assumption
closure, with the relational case carrying an explicit composition premise; centralisation of
invariants as a bound on the read-set, stated relative to those enforcement sites that no
independent evidence settles; a break-even threshold for internal infrastructuralisation under a
stated cost model; a \emph{level family} separating cross-domain isomorphism of the six functions
from transport along problem shape; a \emph{coarse-graining criterion} making the convergence
thesis falsifiable inside a single project; technical debt as a description-length gap; an
\emph{observability} criterion for whether comprehension can converge at all, separating
insufficient observation from insufficient sampling; \emph{governable complexity}
$C=\mathbb{E}[\rho]\cdot\lambda$ against comprehension capacity $K=hb$, giving a stability
condition and a scaling law for required headcount within that model; a Hoeffding bound for
consensus reliability, dominated by a correlated error mode $q$ that is in turn reduced to an
incentive-design problem; and a consolidated assurance condition stated as sufficient rather than
necessary, because its conjuncts are conditions on evidence and a system may be correct without
them. As \emph{falsifiable hypotheses}: the convergence claims, the six-function checklist, the
reading of comprehension as compression, and the cost and scaling trajectories of
Chapter~\ref{ch:program}.

The organising question is not whether individual components can be proved correct, but
\emph{whether guarantees survive the journey} from requirements to executed artefact. A
composition of verified components still fails if the specification omits a requirement, if the
components' environmental assumptions are mutually incompatible, if resource costs are unproved,
if the specification was never authorised by anyone with standing to adopt it, or if the
deployment runs a different artefact from the one verified. Each of those is a distinct failure
with a distinct remedy, and none of them is repaired by proving more components correct.

Fourteen falsifiable hypotheses with operationalisations, controls, falsifiers and risks are
given, together with a six-phase roadmap, a paper-centred interdisciplinary literature map,
and an explicit list of non-claims rejecting the use of G\"odel incompleteness, Rice's
theorem, the open status of $\mathrm{P}$ versus $\mathrm{NP}$, near-decomposability, and
analogies to the Langlands programme or Landauer's principle, as load-bearing universal
laws.
